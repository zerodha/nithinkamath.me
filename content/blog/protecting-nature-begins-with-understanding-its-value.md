I think the only way to protect and start restoring our natural ecosystems is to get governments and people to pay attention to what they are actually worth.

This realization came after talking to people at [Rainmatter Foundation](https://x.com/RainmatterOrg), where we support conservation efforts through CEGS (Centre for Eastern Ghats Studies, part of EBTL). The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) get a lot of attention, but the Eastern Ghats get none.

We have a ~70-acre property next to a forest there, and the diversity of flora & fauna is crazy. We've set up a lab & are inviting students, academics, and other scholars to study it and publish research. The ultimate hope is that we build scientific evidence that helps governments, the public, and other stakeholders understand the value and importance of these critical ecosystems.  

On November 16, we released The Forgotten Ghats, the first CEGS book. [Shreeram M V](https://x.com/mvshreeram) spent ~3 years photographing the landscapes, wildlife, local communities, and the restoration work there.

Something else that worries me is land prices.

A piece of land that earns almost nothing from farming is valued at ₹25 lakh to ₹2–3 crore an acre. But if the returns from farming are barely 1% and the land just keeps appreciating, why would anyone want to farm? No one is then incentivized to nurture the land; instead, it becomes something to speculate on.

The irony is that the more valuable land gets as real estate, the less incentive there is to preserve it as an ecosystem.😬

It is sad that we'll pay crores for an acre but assign almost no value to the biodiversity it supports, the water it recharges, the carbon it stores, or how it regulates the local climate.

As climate change worsens, I think we'll start to see the difference between the price of land & its value.
