It’s that time of the year again when air quality becomes a topic of national discussion because of Delhi. One of the big contributors during this season is stubble burning in Punjab and the surrounding regions. On the worst days, it can account for over a third of Delhi’s pollution.

Delhi gets most of the attention, partly because it has one of the densest networks of air quality sensors. But many cities across North India are just as bad.

Farmers burn stubble because it’s easier and cheaper than the alternatives, like machines that sow the next crop straight through the stubble, sprays that decompose it into manure, or baling it for biomass plants.

But stubble doesn’t have to be waste. It can be turned into biofuel, and farmers can actually make money from it.

India imports nearly 90% of its crude, and that won’t reduce easily. As I’ve said before, biofuels have to be a big part of the solution mix. We need entrepreneurs working on this and solid policy support.

The problem with a lot of these clean fuels is that switching to them means changing a lot of things, from new engines and new equipment to new infrastructure.

That’s what makes [Greenjoules](https://greenjoules.in/), a company we’ve supported through [Rainmatter](https://rainmatter.com), unique. They convert nearly 40 types of agricultural waste and agro-industrial waste into drop-in fuels. Drop-in fuels are not blends, they can totally replace existing fuels. Their fuel has been tested at IIT Madras, ARAI, and IIP Dehradun, and works in existing engines without any changes.

Considering how important this is for a country like India, we’re happy to support them on this journey.
